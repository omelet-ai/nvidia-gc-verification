cuOpt benchmark: warehouse MILP

Result: cuOpt reaches the reported optimum in 15.4 seconds total at 316,814 variables: 1.39–2.86× faster than the compared CPU solvers on this large instance.

Metric
Wall-clock time to optimality; solve time and total time reported separately.
Problem
316,814 variables and 240,120 constraints; warehouse planning/slotting.
Comparison
cuOpt 26.08 on one RTX 4090 versus SCIP 9.0, CP-SAT 9.11 and CBC 2.10 on CPU, 64 threads.
Setup
Xeon Gold 6326 ×2; RTX 4090, 24 GiB (2.0 GiB used as reported); CUDA 13.0; Ubuntu 24.04. Three-run median; solutions independently re-simulated as reported.

Feasibility and scaling

The appendix reports all four solvers reaching the same optimum: 1,139 pallet positions, with 0 constraint violations on re-simulation and a reported 24.3% reduction in peak space. The operational baseline behind the peak-space reduction is not specified.

For an increase from 62K to 317K variables (5.1×), reported time growth is 1.7× for cuOpt, 6.3× for CP-SAT, 7.9× for SCIP and 9.4× for CBC. At smaller, single-warehouse scale, SCIP is reported at 0.2–2.7 seconds. GPU benefit is scale-dependent.

Technical interpretation

The result demonstrates a GPU advantage at the tested problem scale while preserving the reported optimum and feasibility. Reproduction requires the same model instance, solver settings and tolerances; this public report provides aggregate results rather than a downloadable run package.

How to interpret the results

Gap uses a moving, per-instance best-across-solvers reference. Compare distance across time budgets, not the gap percentages as if they shared a fixed global optimum. Aggregated km and mean per-instance gap may rank differently; raw per-instance records are needed to reconstruct the averages.

The appendix reports 2/3 cuOpt wins at both budgets and run-to-run variation: cuOpt ±1.24 / ±0.50 percentage points and RouteOpt-ALNS ±0.87 / ±1.27 percentage points (10 / 60 seconds). The deck describes the frontier difference as within noise. A formal significance/equivalence test and comparable variation for primary RouteOpt are not supplied; no statistical-tie conclusion is independently verified here.

Technical interpretation

Distance reduction versus OR-Tools is calculated as (2,815 − 2,610) / 2,815 = 7.28%. This is a solver comparison on the tested instances, not a customer cost-savings measurement. Customer orders and routes remain confidential.
